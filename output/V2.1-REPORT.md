## 3. The harmonicity-axis fix (V2-REPORT §5) — the core of this run

**The v2 defect, restated plainly.** Axis 2 of the archive measures how *tonal* vs
*noisy* a sound is (spectral flatness). The v2 estimator added a fixed tiny number
(1e-10) to every frequency bin before averaging. For a near-pure tone almost every
bin is genuinely silent, so that average collapsed onto the tiny number itself and
the axis read ~1e-9 — a number about the estimator's arithmetic, not about the
sound. About 6% of random genomes piled onto that floor; the axis went two-humped
and its calibrated scale stretched into meaningless territory, so most ordinary
sounds got squashed into a few bins. That is why archive coverage had fallen to 48%
(V2-REPORT §9b).

**The fix (instrument legibility only — nothing narrowed, nothing rejected).**
- A **relative** noise floor: each bin is floored at one-thousandth of the frame's
  own average power before averaging. A pure tone now reads a *stable* value
  (~1.1e-3) that does not depend on how loud the render is, sitting well above
  estimator noise. A genuinely noisy sound is essentially unchanged.
- **Silent frames are left out of the average** entirely — they carry no harmonic
  information. (The old code counted a silent frame as maximally *noisy*, which
  wrongly pushed silence-dominated creatures to the noisy end.)
- A **belt-and-braces** floor on the calibrated axis minimum (1e-4) so any residual
  near-pure reading still lands cleanly in the tonal edge bin.

**Measured harm p2–p98 over 1,000 random genomes (F10 priors, 4 s renders):**
_before_ p2 = **2.4e-10**, p98 = 1.0 (bimodal, on the estimator floor);
_after_ p2 = **1.13e-3**, p50 = 8.0e-3, p98 = **6.3e-2** — a clean ~1.75-decade
spread with no estimator-floor pile. This is a candidate mis-set constant resolved
exactly as V2-REPORT §5 invited; it is not tuned to make any gate pass (§7).

## 4. Gate 2b — the decision gate — FAILED, and why (the bisect)

Gate 2b is the one gate that can invalidate the archive (BUILD-ORDER, §13.3): the
deep-cell archive assumes an offspring lands **in or near its parent's cell**. Run
at the shipped defaults under the fixed instrument and the F10 priors:

| | p_same (≥0.35) | p_near (≥0.70) | H_cell | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **v2 (last run, N=200)** | 0.567 | 0.779 | 0.333 | PASS |
| **v2.1 (this run, N=200)** | **0.192** | **0.479** | **0.766** | **FAIL** |

Axis calibration this run: dev **[7.55, 304]**, harm **[1.15e-3, 5.11e-2]** (compare
v2's dev [0.014, 308], harm [1.7e-11, 1.0] — both axes moved, see below). Offspring
now scatter far more: p_same more than halved, p_near well under threshold, and
within-cell timbre heterogeneity (H_cell) more than doubled.

**I did not loosen the threshold, retune the instrument, or narrow F10** (each would
be tuning an instrument to a desired answer, §2.2, or a truncation, §2.1). Instead I
bisected (`gates/gate2b-bisect.js`, mutation-only, N=150×20 — a bit noisier/lower
than the N=200 gate, so read the **deltas**, not the absolutes):

| config (activation × harm estimator) | p_same | p_near | H_cell | passes both? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **v2 baseline** (per-slot, old harm) | 0.484 | 0.713 | 0.444 | ~yes (baseline) |
| **instrument-fix alone** (per-slot, v2.1 harm) | 0.387 | **0.634** | 0.678 | **no — p_near** |
| **F10 alone** (F10, old harm) | **0.289** | 0.604 | 0.473 | **no — p_same** |
| **both** (= the failing config) | 0.233 | 0.533 | 0.772 | no |

**Attribution — both changes broke it, in different ways, and they stack:**

- **F10 is the dominant driver of the p_same collapse.** Denser starts (mean ~5.5
  active waves vs ~2.1) make even "static" sounds develop more across their length,
  so the **dev axis min jumps** (0.4 → 2.68 in the bisect; 0.014 → 7.55 in the full
  gate) and the dev range compresses; a denser sound's 8-segment MFCC also varies more
  under a single-wave mutation. F10 alone drops p_same 0.484 → **0.289** (below 0.35).
- **The instrument fix is the dominant driver of the H_cell rise and independently
  sinks p_near.** By making the harmonicity axis *legible* — a genuine second axis
  instead of the degenerate one — offspring now move along harmonicity too, where
  before they were collapsed into one harm bin by the estimator floor. Instrument-fix
  alone: p_same 0.387 (still ≥0.35) but p_near **0.634** (<0.70), H_cell 0.444 → 0.678.

**This is not a bug in either change — it is Gate 2b doing its job.** V2-REPORT §5
warned explicitly that v2's high p_same was *"carried substantially by dev-locality …
the harm axis contributes less than nominal"* because the harm axis was degenerate.
So part of v2's 0.567 was an artefact of a lying instrument collapsing offspring into
the same (wrong) harm bin. Correcting the instrument removes that illusion and reveals
that offspring genuinely move along harmonicity; adding F10's denser starts compresses
the dev axis and adds MFCC variance. The **honest** reading: at 16×16 with these two
MFCC-derived descriptors, the v2.1 kernel+priors do **not** land offspring locally
enough for the deep-grid archive's assumption to hold.

**What §13.3 says to do about it (and what I deliberately did NOT do).** §13.3 names
the legitimate responses to a 2b failure: *a coarser grid, different descriptors, or
adaptive sampling* — an **archive-geometry** change. It does **not** sanction
loosening the gate, quantising the space, or reverting an owner ruling. So this run
**stops here** and hands the geometry decision to the owner (§6). The candidate moves,
none of which narrow the space or add a quality metric:
  1. **Coarser grid** (e.g. 12×12 or 10×10) so a parent's neighbourhood absorbs the
     now-larger offspring spread — the cheapest fix; changes only archive resolution.
  2. **Coarser harm binning** specifically — the legible harm axis is also more
     mutation-mobile; fewer harm bins would restore locality on that axis without
     re-degenerating it.
  3. **Re-examine the descriptors / adaptive sampling** (§13.3) — a larger change.
  4. **Owner reconsiders the F10 range** (e.g. 1..6 instead of 1..10) — their call, as
     it is their ruling; a smaller upper bound would recover much of the p_same drop.

Recommendation: try (1)/(2) first (pure geometry, no space narrowed), re-run Gate 2b,
and only then rebuild/coverage-check the archive. That is an owner-in-the-loop step,
not something this run should decide unilaterally.

## 5. §5.2 prior sanity (1,000 genomes, F10 priors) — no verdict

This check is about the **priors**, not the archive, so it is unaffected by the 2b
result and I ran it (it carries no verdict by design, §5.2). 1,000 random genomes at
60 s / 22.05 kHz under the F10 priors. **Rendered 1000/1000, 0 render errors, 0
non-finite** — synthesis is robust across the F10 prior.

**The near-silent rate falls, exactly as the work order predicted:**

| metric | v2 (per-slot p_active=0.03) | v2.1 (F10, 1..10) |
|---|---|---|
| near-silent (loudness gate) | ~12% | **2.9%** (29/1000) |
| effectively silent (raw RMS < −60 dB) | 127/1000 | 30/1000 |
| silence fraction (median) | 0.73 | **0.037** |
| active waves (min / median / mean / max) | 1 / 2 / 2.11 / 7 | **1 / 6 / 5.65 / 10** |
| onsets (median) | 81 | 215 |
| complexity (median) | 301 | 305 |
| clipped raw (pre-normalisation) | 520/1000 | 741/1000 |

Denser starts mean much less dead air (silence fraction median 0.73 → 0.037) and a
one-in-eight near-silent generation-zero rate drops to about one in thirty-five — a
direct win for interface time and the exact effect the owner wanted from F10, and a
smaller near-silent load for P5's auto-advance to mop up. Active-wave count is the
uniform 1..10 draw (median 6, mean 5.65). More raw clipping (741 vs 520) is expected
from stacking more waves and is fully removed by the §4.7 loudness normaliser (it is a
pre-normalisation readout, not audible). No verdict (§5.2). Full artefact:
`output/gate-artefacts/prior-sanity.json`.

## 6. Why the gate cascade stopped here (Gate 3-plumbing & drift not run)

The work order's Gate 2b step says: *"If it fails, bisect … report which change broke
it, and STOP — do not loosen anything."* I did. Gate 3-plumbing and the drift-control
null model **presuppose a passing decision gate** (BUILD-ORDER: building/measuring the
archive before 2b passes risks throwing it away), so running them now would either
mislead — presenting coverage/drift numbers as if the archive were sound — or need so
much caveating as to be worthless. In particular:

- **The coverage-recovery question** (does 48% climb back toward ~86% now the harm
  axis is legible?) is **moot until the geometry is settled**: coverage is a property
  of whichever grid the owner chooses in §4, and the harm axis is now demonstrably
  non-degenerate (harm [1.15e-3, 5.11e-2], no estimator-floor pile — §3), so it will
  spread genomes better, but reporting a number against a 16×16 grid we have just shown
  is not locality-sound would be a false result.
- **The drift null model** is calibrated to the *shipping* archive; refreshing it
  before the geometry is fixed would produce a baseline for an archive that will not
  ship as-is. It should be regenerated **after** Gate 2b passes on the chosen geometry.

So those two are deferred to the follow-up, explicitly, rather than run for the sake of
completeness. Nothing here was loosened to manufacture a pass.

## 7. Where narrowing was tempting — and what was done instead

- **The harm axis read badly, and the easy "fix" would have been to reshape the
  distribution until coverage looked good.** Instead the estimator's numerical floor
  was corrected (a legibility fix), and the constant was chosen from what makes a
  pure tone read stably — not from what makes a gate pass. Coverage is reported as
  measured, whatever it is.
- **P5 near-silent creatures.** The owner's instinct is to delete them at creation;
  that is a §2.1-forbidden truncation and was **not** done. Only the *presentation*
  is handled (auto-advance), and the true short dwell is still recorded. Selection is
  untouched; nothing is deleted. Option D (selection bias against near-silence) stays
  vetoed (V2-REPORT §9).
- **F10 wave count.** Made an explicit 1..10 range, not a cap: every count and slot
  combination stays reachable, the kill-switch stays evolvable, the floor is
  untouched. A bet on where to start, not on what exists.
- **Favourites (F11) are firewalled.** A star is stated preference; nothing reads the
  favourites store into fitness, descriptors or selection (§8.6). The temptation to
  "learn from what the owner likes" is exactly the project's failure mode and was not
  taken.
- **W1 forced density judges nothing** and changes no prior — `opts.nActive` forces a
  count for that batch only; the shipped F10 draw (1..10) is unchanged.
